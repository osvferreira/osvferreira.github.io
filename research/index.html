The QCD epoch, pion condensation and gravitational waves

About a microsecond after the Big Bang, the quark-gluon plasma filling the Universe turned into hadrons. In the standard picture this QCD transition is a smooth crossover. However, the lepton asymmetries of the early Universe are only weakly constrained, and if they were large the Universe may have crossed a different region of the QCD phase diagram. Using the quark-meson model, we showed that large but physically motivated lepton asymmetries can drive the Universe into a pion-condensed phase through a first-order phase transition, a new possible source of primordial gravitational waves. Large tau-lepton flavor asymmetries can also push the speed of sound above its conformal value, which leaves a distinctive imprint on the low-frequency gravitational-wave spectrum. This can be tested with pulsar timing arrays such as NANOGrav.

Papers: Phys. Rev. Lett. 137, 151001 (2026) · Phys. Rev. D 112, 094009 (2025)

Compact stars as probes of dense matter and dark matter

Neutron stars and their more exotic relatives reach densities several times larger than those of atomic nuclei, which makes them natural laboratories for cold and dense QCD matter. I have studied strange quark stars admixed with fermionic dark matter in the presence of magnetic fields of order 10¹⁷–10¹⁸ G, solving the two-fluid Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkov equations. Among the observables, the tidal deformability, which is accessible through gravitational waves from neutron-star mergers, is by far the most affected by dark matter and magnetic fields.

Papers: JCAP 04 (2023) 012

Electromagnetic response of hot and dense QCD matter

How does the quark-gluon plasma respond to electric and magnetic fields? I compute the electromagnetic susceptibilities of hot and dense matter from first principles. This includes the power corrections to the photon polarization tensor in a medium of massive fermions, and the leading hard and soft perturbative-QCD corrections. Matching to lattice QCD at zero baryon density lets us extend these results to finite density, where lattice methods do not apply. These densities are relevant for intermediate-energy heavy-ion collisions.

Papers: JHEP 11 (2026) 034 · Phys. Rev. D 109, 016025 (2024)

Bayesian inference for heavy-ion collisions

At IF-USP I work on the Bayesian calibration of hybrid hydrodynamic models of heavy-ion collisions, as part of the ExTrEMe collaboration. A systematic analysis of the full parameter space of a state-of-the-art model shows that no parameter set describes the low-transverse-momentum pion spectra measured by ALICE. This points to a missing mechanism that enhances soft-pion production.

Papers: arXiv:2611.18290
