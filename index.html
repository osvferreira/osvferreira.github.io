Osvaldo Ferreira

Postdoctoral researcher · Institute of Physics, University of São Paulo

🇧🇷 Versão em português

I am a postdoctoral researcher at the Institute of Physics of the University of São Paulo (IF-USP). My research sits at the interface of nuclear physics, astrophysics and cosmology. I study strongly interacting matter under extreme conditions, and how to observe it. In the early Universe, I investigate the QCD transition and its signatures in the cosmological gravitational-wave background. In astrophysics, I use compact stars as probes of matter at very high densities. I also compute properties of hot and dense matter, such as its response to electromagnetic fields, using perturbative QCD.

More recently, I have been applying Bayesian inference to the phenomenology of heavy-ion collisions, contributing to the ExTrEMe (EXperiment and TheoRy in Extreme MattEr) collaboration. You can read more on the research page or download my CV.

Interests

  • QCD phase diagram and the QCD epoch of the early Universe
  • Cosmological gravitational-wave background
  • Compact stars, dense matter and dark matter
  • Hot and dense QCD with perturbative methods
  • Bayesian inference for heavy-ion collisions

Recent publications

Are hybrid hydrodynamic models missing soft-pion physics?
O. Ferreira, T. S. Domingues, F. G. Gardim, et al. · arXiv:2611.18290, 2026

A Bayesian analysis shows that state-of-the-art hybrid hydrodynamic models cannot reproduce the excess of soft pions measured by ALICE, pointing to non-equilibrium physics missing from current simulations.

Signatures from Pion Condensation and Lepton Flavor Asymmetries in the Cosmological Gravitational Wave Background
O. Ferreira, E. S. Fraga, J. Schaffner-Bielich · Phys. Rev. Lett. 137, 151001, 2026

Large tau-lepton asymmetries could drive the early Universe into a pion-condensed phase, leaving a distinctive imprint on the nanohertz gravitational-wave background probed by pulsar timing arrays.
