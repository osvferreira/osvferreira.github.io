The asymmetries between leptons and antileptons in the early Universe are only weakly constrained by observations. If the asymmetry in the tau flavor was large during the QCD epoch, about a microsecond after the Big Bang, the Universe may have passed through a phase of **pion condensation**. In this phase the speed of sound exceeds its conformal value, c<sub>s</sub><sup>2</sup> &gt; 1/3, producing a peak in the equation of state instead of the usual dip at the QCD crossover.

The low-frequency part of a gravitational-wave background from causal sources is sensitive to the equation of state at the time each mode re-enters the horizon, so this peak leaves a distinctive imprint on the spectrum. We computed the resulting spectrum and its tilt, compared them with the standard case without lepton asymmetry, and discussed the implications for the stochastic background reported by pulsar timing arrays, using the NANOGrav 15-year dataset. Detecting or ruling out this signature in future data would constrain lepton asymmetries in the early Universe.
